# STR Search: Red Room research findings

*Research updated October 27, 2026. Revenue = AirDNA Revenue Potential, last 12 months: modeled revenue, not actual income.*

**The question:** Do adult-themed Airbnbs earn more than comparable conventional properties, and what drives it?

**The answer: Yes, in the right cabin. The theme multiplies a strong getaway.** A themed hot-tub cabin in a mountain or lake getaway market earns about 50% more modeled revenue than comparable conventional cabins, and it is the only profile that regularly reaches $100K. City units earn far less ($37–49K medians), and themed listings without a hot tub only match their comps.

- **+50.0%** median gap for 15 confirmed cabin properties vs. photo-matched conventional cabins (13 of 15 above; median $33,661).
- **+50.0%** median gap for the 41 of 56 listings across 40 cities that have reviewed comps (31 above, 10 below). Broad citywide comparisons overstate this (+91.5%).
- The two datasets use different AirDNA pulls and are reported separately, never pooled.

## Question 1: Which traditional features are needed?

**The full cabin-getaway package.**

- Required: a private hot tub. No listing without one reaches $100K.
- A cabin, A-frame or treehouse in a mountain/lake destination ($94K median vs. $46K in cities).
- Updated finishes (3/5 or better), a fire pit and outdoor dining.
- Helpful, not required: views, a sauna, a spa bathroom.

## Question 2: Which kink amenities and design patterns?

**A credible core kit, not a maximal dungeon.**

- Core kit: a swing, a St. Andrew’s cross, a spanking bench and bed restraint points, in red/LED lighting.
- Swing (+43%) and cross (+38%) carry the clearest signal among comparable cabins; a cage and neon are upgrades.
- No real equipment: −25% vs. comps. One to three pieces: already +71%.
- Skip the stripper pole. Tantra chairs are common but show no edge.

## Question 3: What else goes with stronger performance?

**Contained play space, the getaway photo and the operator.**

- Keep play in one dedicated room or the main bedroom ($107–112K vs. $79K when scattered).
- Lead listing photos with the exterior, view or hot tub ($132K vs. $79K).
- Two bedrooms edge out one ($117K vs. $101K); 3–4 bedroom party houses lag.
- Branded repeat operators reach $130–183K. Oversized master bedrooms are not supported by the data.

- **Hot tub:** The gate. No listing without one reaches $100K, and those listings only match their own comps.
- **Cabin in a getaway market:** Hot-tub city houses, apartments and suites top out around $49K. A cabin, A-frame or treehouse in a mountain/lake destination moves the median up.
- **A real kit + decent finishes:** The first rung where $100K is likely. Hot-tub cabins with only a lit “secret room” all stay under $100K.
- **Operator execution:** Within rung 3, the $100K+ listings run $391 ADR and 83% occupancy vs. $307 and 66%. Seven of the 11 come from operators running 2–3 adult listings.

## What to buy

- A 1–2 bedroom cabin or A-frame with a private hot tub in a proven mountain/lake getaway market.
- Room for one dedicated play space alongside a normal sleeping bedroom.
- The core kit, decent finishes and a cover photo that sells the setting first.
- An operator with premium pricing discipline: the top listings run about $390+ ADR at 80%+ occupancy.
- Underwrite against premium conventional cabins in the same market, never against a citywide average.

## Limits

- A discovered set of 56 listings, not a random sample; higher rungs contain only 4–20 listings each.
- Revenue Potential is modeled and flatters young listings (15 of 56 have fewer than 300 active nights).
- Gaps are associations with a few reviewed comps, not causal theme effects or return estimates. 18 listings have no usable comp.
- Photo coding reflects public galleries on October 27, 2026; not shown does not mean absent.
- The 56-listing set uses a newer AirDNA pull than the 15 confirmed-cabin comparisons; the two are reported separately.
- Directional evidence. Both samples were discovered rather than randomly drawn, revenue is AirDNA’s modeled figure and comp sets are small. The gap is an observed association, not a guaranteed uplift.
